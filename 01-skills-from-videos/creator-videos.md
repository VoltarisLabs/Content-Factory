# What each creator video taught us

## 3D motion graphics with Claude (AIpreneur)
The video shows Claude planning and writing glossy 3D clay objects with bold kinetic words, scene by scene, for free.
**What we learned:** the style is good for explaining an idea, but don't open on it. In our numbers, drawn graphics on the first frame skipped 60 to 79% (see [the first second](../02-hooks-and-openers/the-first-second.md)).

## Dynamic keyword captions (@jayapapa_ya)
Big coloured ALL CAPS word chunks that pop in time with the voice.
**What we learned:** short word chunks keep pace with the voice. We later moved to the three tier style below.

## Three tier captions and circle wipes (@creatornvnn)
One big keyword sits between two small lines, and wipes are used only when the picture changes to a different kind of shot.
**What we learned:** one big word per beat is easier to read than a full line of text. This became our caption style on 2026-10-09.

## Five re-hook sentence devices (Chris Chung)
Five ways to phrase a line so it opens a question the sentence doesn't close:
1. Show the result first, then how.
2. "It's not A or B, it's actually C."
3. State a common belief, then contradict it.
4. "But here's the better way."
5. "This won't work, unless..."

**What we learned:** watch time tracks reach on our account, and re-hooks are the cheapest way to move it. We haven't proven them on our own posts yet.

## Token budget audit (Sharbel A.)
How to find where an AI coding assistant's usage actually goes.
**What we learned:** when we measured, 97.8% of our usage was re-reading old context, not new work. Measure first, then clear the session between jobs.

## Live dashboards from a question (Brennan Wells)
Ask a question in plain words and get a dashboard that shows how each number was worked out.
**What we learned:** showing how each number was worked out is what makes a dashboard trustworthy.

## The "codes" reel format (@mavgpt)
Each line names a prompt "code" and shows its finished result. Top reels reached 283K and 231K views.
**What we learned:** the account posts 3 to 4 times a day and most of those posts flop. It tests one topic several ways and reposts the winners, and the payload is usable the same day with no coding. Its winners run 52 to 60 seconds, which goes against our own numbers, so we test its ideas before adopting them.

## Proof cards over a raw selfie (@mmaximus.soares)
A raw selfie video, with a small proof card landing on each claim.
**What we learned:** a talking head becomes believable when every claim has proof on screen. An honest "what it won't do" line builds even more trust.
